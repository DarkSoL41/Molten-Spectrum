ABOUT THE GAME

Molten Spectrum is a turn-based puzzle game for the ZX Spectrum 48K/128K. It plays like Sokoban — you push ingots around a grid — but the ingots are metals. Melt them in pots to make alloys, fill the moulds of each order, clean metal in acid, win copper from vitriol, and in the later shops load lasers with metal: every beam carries the colours of its metal, opens spectral doors and melts metal in cold pots.

The old Red Crucible Works is back in business. You are its new smelter: take the orders of fifteen shops, from the casting shop to the spectral lab and the night shifts, and become the master of the crucible.

There is no time limit, no lives and no randomness. Every level was checked by a solver, every move can be undone, and every level has a par — the shortest known solution. Solve a level in par and you earn a MASTER mark.

  • 150 levels in 15 chapters (80 levels in 8 chapters on a 48K machine)
  • 6 metals, 6 alloys, real recipes: bronze, brass, steel, cupronickel, nickel silver, stainless steel
  • acid and vitriol baths, one-way gates, lasers, mirrors, prisms, spectrometers, spectral doors, cold pots
  • smooth movement, 512 moves of undo, a graphical in-game manual
  • original AY techno soundtrack on 128K, beeper effects on every machine
  • a four-letter code for every level

GETTING STARTED

The game is one tape file, MoltenSpectrum.tap. Open it in any ZX Spectrum emulator (Fuse, Spectaculator, Unreal Speccy, ZEsarUX, Retro Virtual Machine…) or load it on a real machine with LOAD "". The game finds out by itself whether it runs on a 48K or a 128K machine.

THE SCREEN

The room takes the upper part of the screen, the status panel the lower four lines:

  1. LEVEL 1-01 FIRST BRONZE — chapter, level and the name of the order.
  2. MOVES — your moves so far, PAR — the shortest known solution, CODE — the code of this level.
  3. The recipes of the chapter, e.g. CU+SN=BZ.
  4. A hint (first levels of a chapter) or a reminder of the keys.

On levels with spectrometers two lines show, for spectrometer I and II, the TARGET colours and what it sees NOW, and OK when they match.

Before each level its name is shown; press a key (or wait 5 seconds).

RULES

The goal

A level is solved when every mould holds an ingot of its material and, if the room has spectrometers, every spectrometer is happy.

Moving and pushing

  • You walk one cell at a time. Walking into an ingot (or a mirror, or a prism) pushes it one cell — if the cell behind it lets it in.
  • You push only one thing at a time and you cannot pull. An ingot pushed into a corner stays there.
  • Walls, baths, lasers, spectrometers, shut doors, hot pots and gates against their arrow stop you.
  • You may never end a move standing in a beam — such a move is refused (you hear a short burn).

What happens to a pushed ingot

It is pushed into…Result
floor, a mould, an open door, a gate along its arrowit moves
an empty potit melts: the pot now holds molten metal
a pot with molten metalif the two make a recipe, a solid ingot of the alloy appears in the pot (push it out); otherwise the push is refused
an acid bathCu or C: sinks and fills the bath, which becomes floor. Anything else dissolves and disappears.
a vitriol bathSn, Zn, Fe or Ni: the bath disappears and a Cu ingot lies in its place. Other ingots are refused.
a laser socketit lies in the socket and the laser shines its colours
a cold potdark: it just lies there. In a beam: it melts or alloys like in a hot pot.

Mirrors and prisms are glass: they cannot go into pots, baths or sockets.

Undo and restart

Every move can be undone (up to 512 moves back) — including melting, dissolving and alloying. There is no way to lose: if a level is stuck, undo or restart it.

LIGHT AND OPTICS

Beams

  • A laser shines only while an ingot lies in its socket, in the direction away from the socket. The beam has the colours of that ingot: C magenta, Fe yellow, Cu red (warm) and Zn cyan, Sn blue, Ni green (cold). An alloy beam shows stripes of all its colours.
  • A beam passes floor, moulds, pots (empty or molten), gates, baths, sockets, cold pots and open doors.
  • It is stopped by walls, solid ingots and shut doors. Molten metal lets it through, and so does any metal in a cold pot.
  • Beams cross each other freely. After every move all beams are traced again.
  • You can never stand in a beam. A beam across a room can cut you off — keep a way round.

Mirrors

A mirror turns a beam by 90°: / sends a beam going right upwards, a beam going up to the right; \ sends a beam going right downwards. Both faces reflect, so one mirror can serve two beams.

The prism

A prism splits a beam by temperature: warm colours (Cu, Fe, C) turn left, cold colours (Zn, Sn, Ni) turn right, as seen along the beam. A bronze beam (Cu + Sn) becomes a red beam to the left and a blue one to the right. A purely warm beam just turns left.

Spectrometers and doors

  • A spectrometer adds up all beams that hit it, from any side. It is happy only when it sees exactly its target colours: a missing colour and an extra colour are equally wrong.
  • A target can be reached in different ways: a bronze laser, or a copper beam and a tin beam meeting at the spectrometer.
  • A spectral door with the same numeral (I or II) is open while its spectrometer is happy, and also while something stands in it: an ingot left in a doorway keeps the door open.

Cold pots

A cold pot is floor in the dark. When a beam (of any colour) falls on it, it becomes a hot pot: what lies in it melts, a second metal alloys with it. Take the light away — pull the ingot out of the laser socket or put an ingot in the beam’s way to cast a shadow — and the metal sets as a solid ingot that you can push out.

PAR, MASTER AND CODES

  • PAR is the shortest solution the solver knows. For almost every level it is the proven minimum; for a few of the hardest levels it is the best solution found.
  • Solve a level in par or fewer moves and the CAST COMPLETE window shows MASTER.
  • The window also shows the code of the next level. Write the codes down: the game keeps your results only while the machine is on.
  • After the tenth level of a chapter you see the chapter summary: your best moves for each level and the number of MASTER marks. The finale counts the MASTER marks of all chapters.
  • Any level can be started from the title menu: 2 CODE, the four letters, ENTER.

TIPS

  • Before the first push, look at every mould and ask: which ingot goes there, and from which side can it be pushed in?
  • A pot is a one-way door for metal: what goes in does not come out until it is an alloy. A pure metal left alone in the only pot blocks that pot.
  • An alloy is born inside the pot — make sure you can stand on the far side to push it out. A pot by a wall needs a plan for the exit.
  • An ingot against a wall can only slide along the wall; an ingot in a corner is lost. Sometimes that is exactly what you want for a spare ingot.
  • Not every ingot is needed. Spare metal can be dissolved in acid, parked in a corner, or used as a weight that keeps a door open.
  • The ingot in a laser socket is a key: while it shines, the door is open. If the same ingot is needed in a mould, think about when to take it.
  • A solid ingot is a shadow. Put one in a beam to cool a cold pot or to close a door behind you.
  • Undo is free. Experiment, look, undo.

TECHNICAL NOTES

  • Written in Z80 assembly (sjasmplus). One tape for both machines: BASIC loader, loading screen, the 128K level bank, the main code.
  • On 128K the levels of chapters 9–15 are kept in RAM bank 1; the game detects 128K by itself and falls back to 80 levels on a 48K machine.
  • Music: AY-3-8912 (128K, title, chapter screens, finale). Sound effects: beeper, on every machine. A 48K with an AY interface also plays the music.
  • Rooms of up to 16×10 cells of 16×16 pixels, smooth movement at 4 pixels per frame, 50 frames per second.
  • Every level was solved by a computer solver that follows the game rules exactly; the par values come from it. All 150 levels are replayed move by move in an emulator test against a model of the rules.
  • Tested in emulators (48K and 128K). Other models (+2A/+3, Pentagon…) are expected to work but have not been tested on real hardware.
